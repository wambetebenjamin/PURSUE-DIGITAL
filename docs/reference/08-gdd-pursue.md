# 08 — GDD: PURSUE

**Working title:** PURSUE — *for you shall surely overtake them, and without
fail recover all* (1 Samuel 30:8)

**One line:** a city-building strategy game about rebuilding a burned city and
then going out to take back what was stolen — where the city is the game, and
the march is one button away.

## 1. Premise

David has been outlawed and living in the wilderness. The Amelekites have
raided Ziklag and burned it, taking the women, the children, the goods and the
livestock. David finds the abandoned goats at the brook, crosses the
Wadi, and comes to the ruined city. He speaks to the men at the gate, and they
refuse. He sends them out: *"Pursue, for you shall surely overtake them, and
without fail recover all."* They go. By dusk they are back, and the city is
their own again.

The game is that sentence, playable.

- **The city** is what you build while you wait for the men to come back.
- **The march** is what they do.
- **The two are the same game**, because the city decides what the march can
  do.

## 2. The player fantasy

Not a hero. A **commander of a city that is still standing.** You decide who
goes, what they carry, and what the city becomes when they return. David is
present but rarely central — the men are the instrument, the city is the
subject.

## 3. Core loop

```
        ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
        │                                              │
   rebuild the Works ──► raise companies ──► send the march
        ▲                        │                    │
        │                        ▼                    ▼
   the spoil returns ◄── the city grows ◄── the field fight
        │                                              │
        └──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The cycle is 8–15 minutes at a sitting, and it never stops: production ticks
whether you are watching or not, the road is always there, and the next hold
is always one march away.

### Session shape

1. **Open on the city** (already the case — the title card stands over the
   walled plateau). Collect what the Works have produced.
2. **Spend** — raise a Work, recruit a company, equip a hero.
3. **Choose a hold** on the Chart — the road south.
4. **March** — the encounter plays out; skills are manual where they matter.
5. **Return** — the spoil lands, the Works fill, the city is visibly more
   than it was.

## 4. The two screens

### The City

An isometric walled plateau, seen from above. Octagonal curtain wall, drum
towers, a gatehouse, a gate road, a cross way, a ring road, a plaza with a
well and market awnings. Seven Works inside the walls, each on its own site,
each visibly different at every level. Fourteen people walk the streets;
sheep in the fold; smoke from the smithy; the mill's blades turn.

Seven Works:

| Work | Lv 1 | Lv 5 | Gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Companies** (barracks) | a timber shed | a stone hall with an armoury and wings | Levies → Retainers → Champions |
| **The Granary** | a threshing floor | a stone storehouse, full | food cap |
| **The Quarry** | a cut in the rock | a worked face with a crane | stone |
| **The Smithy** | a lean-to with a hearth | a proper forge, ringing | equipment |
| **The Watch** (wall) | a timber palisade | a crenellated stone wall with lit braziers | the marches |
| **The Ark** (hall) | a tent on the ridge | a great hall with banners and a gold finial | the charge |
| **The Hall of Ways** (chart) | a post | a lit war-room | the Chart |

Each Work has **five levels**, and raising it changes the building on screen —
not just a number. That is the promise of this game: the city is the save
file.

### The March

The pursuit on foot, in third person, over real ground — scrub, rock, the
wadi, the road. You are David and his men: they fight, and where the fight
has a decision you make, you make it fast.

Three flavours of hold, borrowed from the reference's structure:

| Flavour | What it is | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Stealth** | a small camp; avoid it, or take it | sneak past, or strike at dusk |
| **Dungeon** | a place (a cave, a storehouse, a yard) | three heroes, no troops, skills on a gauge |
| **Field** | a company marching out | heroes + troops, formation, largely automatic |

## 5. The chart

The map of the road south, drawn on vellum: Ziklag at the top, the brook, the
wadi, the Arabah, the Amelekite holds scattered along the way. Each hold is a
node. Node states: **held** (a small stamp of your seal), **known** (you have
heard of it), **rumoured** (a question mark, only visible after a scout
returns). Contiguous holds must fall before the next opens — the map is a
campaign, not a checklist.

Stars: **swift**, **silent**, **unseen** — the three ways to take a hold.
Silent and unseen need heroes with the right skills, and skills are the scarce
thing.

## 6. Progression

### Resources

| Resource | From | Spent on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Grain** | the Granary | companies, marches |
| **Timber** | the Quarry | Work levels |
| **Silver** | holds, the market | Works, heroes, equipment |
| **Men** | the Companies | the army |

Four resources, each from one place, each with one obvious use. No
soft-currency soup.

### The seven phases of the charge (1 Samuel 30)

The campaign is the biblical seven, and each phase opens a new system rather
than a new number:

| Phase | The chapter | What it opens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The abandoned goats (1 Sam 30:17–18) | the first march, the first hold |
| 2 | The refusal at the gate (v. 19–22) | the Companies, and the choice to go anyway |
| 3 | The brook and the wadi (v. 24–26) | the Watch, the scout, fog on the chart |
| 4 | The Arabah (v. 27–31) | heroes, and the first real loss |
| 5 | The host of the Amelekites (v. 32–33) | the Champions, and the wall matters |
| 6 | The pursuit (v. 34–36) | the swift and silent stars |
| 7 | Recovered all (v. 37–38) | the city at full height; the season closes |

### Levels

- **Works**: 1–5, each visibly different.
- **Heroes**: 1–10, each with a skill at 5 and another at 10.
- **Companies**: three tiers, unlocked by the Companies' level.
- **The Ark (hall)**: 1–5 — the charge itself; each level adds a phase.

## 7. The mighty men

Heroes come from 2 Samuel 23 — the thirty-seven mighty men — plus the men of
1 Samuel 30:9, Joab's men. Four classes:

| Class | Name in game | What they do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mighty** | the mighty men | strength; break a line |
| **Swift** | the runners | speed; take the road, arrive first |
| **Silent** | the quiet ones | stealth; the silent star |
| **Unseen** | the scouts | sight; reveal the chart |

A hero roster of **twelve** for launch, each with one signature skill, and
**bonds**: two heroes who served together in the war-room of Gibeah, or in
the cave of Adullam, or in the fight at the brook, are stronger when both are
in the same company. Bonds are the depth mechanic, and they cost nothing but
attention.

## 8. Art direction

**Not** a modern UI on a fantasy skin. A **biblical, sun-bleached, living
world**: dry stone, ochre, olive, dust, and the deep blue of a night march.
No neon, no glass panels, no all-caps sci-fi labels. Serif type with a
Semitic cadence — the labels read like scripture, not like a spreadsheet.

Per-scene colour temperature:

| Scene | Light | Palette |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ziklag at noon | high, hard | bleached limestone, white dust, deep shadow |
| Ziklag at dusk | low, amber | ochre walls, long violet shadows, brazier orange |
| The brook | cool, green | olive scrub, wet rock, cold water |
| The wadi | harsh white | glare, heat shimmer, dust |
| The Arabah | dusk, red | red rock, low sun, long shadows |
| Night march | moonlight, blue | blue-grey, torch orange only |

**Typography:** a readable serif (EB Garamond or Frank Ruhl Libre for Hebrew
terms), generous leading, no tracked-out capitals, no drop-shadow.

## 9. Sound

Wind, goats, the creak of a cart, a smith's hammer, a horn in the wadi.
Biblical Hebrew terms are spoken sparingly and only for names. No musical score
under the march — the silence of a pursuit is the point.

## 10. What we are not doing

To be explicit, so the scope holds:

- **No gacha, no random packs, no premium currency.** Silver is earned.
- **No energy timers gating play.** Time gates *production* (which is the
  genre's engine) but never gate *playing*.
- **No pay-to-win.** Nothing purchasable touches a hold's difficulty.
- **No season pass as a grind.** The covenant season is a *story* chapter with
  a balance retune, free for everyone.
- **No alliances in v1.** The tribe is a roadmap item, not a promise.

## 11. The first five minutes

1. The title card stands over the walled city. Nothing else on screen.
2. **Enter Ziklag.** The camera settles over the plateau. The city is already
   standing, already alive — walkers, smoke, the mill turning.
3. The lord's bar: David, the current charge, grain, timber, silver, men.
4. Tap **the Companies**. A card: level pips, what the level is worth, the
   cost. **Raise the work** — and the barracks on screen grows a storey.
5. Tap **the Chart**. The road south, vellum and ink. Three holds. The first
   one is outlined in red.
6. **Take the road.** The camera drops to the ground, and the march begins.

That is the game. Everything else is depth on top of it.
